Where do meteorites

Follow the stone from the young Solar System to the morning it lit up the sky.

  • ~4.6 BILLION YEARS AGO
    Dust becomes rock

    Dust and rock gathered around the young Sun. Some of that early material became part of asteroids. NASA

  • EARLY SOLAR SYSTEM
    An asteroid holds the clues

    Chelyabinsk is an LL5 ordinary chondrite. Its minerals and rounded grains preserve clues about its rocky parent body. Sample record

  • LATER · DATE UNKNOWN
    A fragment breaks away

    Collisions can knock pieces off asteroids. The exact time and source of the Chelyabinsk fragment remain uncertain. NASA

  • BEFORE 15 FEBRUARY 2013
    A path to Earth

    The fragment orbited the Sun. It met Earth when both reached the same point in space at the same time. Trajectory study

A FRAGMENT IN ORBIT

Once separated, a fragment follows its own orbit around the Sun. Over time gravitational encounters can change where that orbit takes it.

AN ENCOUNTER WITH EARTH

Earth and the fragment reached the same point at the same time. The rock entered the atmosphere and broke apart.

AFTER THE AIRBURST
Some fragments
reach the ground.
The surviving pieces are meteorites. The main mass from Chelyabinsk was recovered from Lake Chebarkul.

STONE AND METAL

Stony meteorites are rich in silicate minerals. Iron meteorites are mostly iron and nickel. Stony-iron meteorites contain substantial amounts of both silicate and metal.

THE CHELYABINSK SAMPLE

Chelyabinsk is an LL5 ordinary chondrite. The small rounded grains typical of chondrites are called chondrules. Its interior was also altered by heat before the fall.
